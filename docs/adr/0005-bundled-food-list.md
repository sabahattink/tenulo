# ADR 0005: Bundled food list from USDA SR Legacy

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-11-16

## Context

The 1.1 food diary (docs/specs/2026-11-16-daily-log.md) needs a searchable list of foods with
energy and nutrients, in Turkish and English. Tenulo makes no network calls (AGENTS.md, the
privacy policy), so there is no online food database and no barcode lookup. First market is
Türkiye, so the list must know menemen, mercimek çorbası and simit, not just raw ingredients.
Every number must be traceable to a public source; nothing is typed in from memory.

## Decision

- The list ships inside the app as `src/domain/foods/catalog.generated.ts`, generated by
  `scripts/foods/build_foods.py` (Python standard library only) from two committed inputs:
  `scripts/foods/foods.json` (the curated list) and `scripts/foods/usda-sr-extract.csv` (the SR
  rows it uses: fdc_id, description and the five nutrients per 100 g, copied verbatim).
- Source: USDA FoodData Central "SR Legacy" (April 2018), public domain under CC0 1.0. It is the
  final release of the Standard Reference database and is never revised, so fdc_ids and values
  are stable, and it covers generic foods rather than products. Downloaded from
  fdc.nal.usda.gov/download-datasets as `FoodData_Central_sr_legacy_food_csv_2018-04.zip`
  (sha256 `b80817294b8850530aaedf2e515c02593b1824f763a0ff356e5c2081643e6fd0`). The full
  download is not committed; the extract is (223 rows). Nutrient ids: energy 1008 (kcal),
  protein 1003, carbohydrate by difference 1005, total fat 1004, total dietary fibre 1079; the
  generator checks them against `nutrient.csv` when it rebuilds the extract.
- A simple food is one SR row. Where SR lacks the Turkish food, the closest SR food stands in and
  the food's `note` says so: kaşar is provolone, lor is part-skim ricotta, kefir is cultured
  whole buttermilk, pekmez is cane molasses, sucuk is cured smoked beef sausage, çipura is scup
  (same sea bream family), leblebi is dry chickpeas, Türk kahvesi is espresso.
- A dish is a home-style recipe: SR ingredients in raw grams plus the cooked yield in grams,
  water included on both sides, with a one-line `note`. Per 100 g of the dish =
  Σ(ingredient per 100 g × grams / 100) × 100 / yield. Nutrients other than water are taken as
  retained; the yield carries evaporation, water taken up by rice, bulgur or boiled dough, and
  frying oil taken up (stated per recipe). Where meat dominates and SR has the cooked cut (köfte,
  döner), the cooked SR food is used so drip losses are SR's own. A recipe may use another food of
  the list (`"@lavash"`). The generator refuses a yield above the raw weight or below 30 % of it.
- Arithmetic is exact (Python `Decimal`), rounded once at the end: energy to whole kcal,
  nutrients to whole milligrams, half away from zero.
- Portions are household units with whole grams, default first: SR's own portion weights where
  they fit (a large egg 50 g, a medium banana 118 g), otherwise typical Turkish measures (su
  bardağı 200 ml, çay bardağı 100 ml, a slice of somun 25 g). The generator adds "100 g" and
  "1 g" to every food. Liquids are weighed at 1 g per ml; milk is about 3 % denser and oil about
  8 % lighter, well inside the uncertainty of a household portion.
- No brand names: only generic SR rows are used, and the generator refuses any SR description
  with a capitalised word (SR's convention for brands).
- Ids are stable kebab-case English ids. Logged entries store a snapshot of name, portion and
  nutrients (spec, `food_entry`), so regenerating the list never changes history. An id is never
  reused for a different food; a removed food simply stops being found (`findCatalogFood`).
- Search (`src/domain/foods/search.ts`) folds case and Turkish letters ("kofte" finds "Köfte",
  "IZGARA" equals "ızgara"), matches names and aliases in both languages with the current
  language first, ranks whole words above partial ones, exact and prefix above word starts above
  substrings, and recent foods first among equal matches. An empty query returns recent foods.
- Checks: the generator validates the spec (unique ids, both names, positive integer portions,
  every reference resolves, plausible yields) and `--check` fails when the module is stale.
  `catalog.test.ts` checks integrity and that each food's energy agrees with 4/4/9 kcal per gram
  of protein/carbohydrate/fat (fibre at 2) within max(20 kcal, 10 %), the spread of SR's
  food-specific Atwater factors.

## Consequences

- Values are estimates. SR describes US foods; Turkish produce, cheeses, breeds and recipes
  differ, and a dish depends on the cook, the oil and the plate. Portions are typical home
  portions, not weighed standards. Tenulo shows these numbers as the user's own log and never as
  advice.
- Foods SR cannot represent honestly are left out rather than guessed: şalgam, tarhana, kaymak,
  boza, and a generic protein bar (SR has only branded bars). Users add these as their own foods.
- Amounts in grams use the 1 g portion, so `food_entry.quantity_milli` must allow gram counts in
  thousandths; the 1.1 spec's 1–100000 caps such an entry at 100 g.
- Regenerate: `python scripts/foods/build_foods.py`. After changing which SR foods are used, first
  download and unzip the SR Legacy CSV, then
  `python scripts/foods/build_foods.py --sr-dir <unzipped folder>` to rebuild the extract.
- Adding or changing a food means editing `foods.json` (an SR food or a recipe with its note,
  names, aliases, portions), regenerating, running `pnpm test`, and committing the spec, the
  extract and the generated module together.
